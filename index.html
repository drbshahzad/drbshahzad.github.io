About Me

A career built across four countries, on one continuing question: how do we make software engineering more sustainable, more trustworthy, and more human?

Dr. Basit Shahzad is an Associate Professor of Software Engineering at the University of Prince Mugrin, Saudi Arabia. He holds a Ph.D. in Software Engineering from Universiti Teknologi PETRONAS (UTP), Malaysia, and completed postdoctoral research at the Computer Laboratory, University of Cambridge, United Kingdom.

His academic path has taken him through Pakistan, Malaysia, the UK, and Saudi Arabia, with visiting fellowships at Macquarie University (Australia) and Hagenberg (Austria). His research agenda centers on software architecture, green and sustainable computing, AI-assisted software engineering, and risk and cost estimation within requirements engineering — with a consistent underlying commitment to humanized computing: using technology deliberately in service of people.

Teaching & Mentorship

Dr. Shahzad teaches within the undergraduate Software Engineering program at the University of Prince Mugrin, with a focus on requirements engineering and, more recently, agentic software engineering practice.

SE 471 — Agentic Software Engineering

Covers agent architectures, the Model Context Protocol (MCP), and frameworks including LangGraph, CrewAI, and AutoGen, alongside prompt- and spec-driven engineering methods and the ethical questions raised by autonomous software agents. Taught using Modern Software Engineering with AI Essentials as the core reference.

SE 311 — Software Requirements Engineering

A revised specification (V3) integrating named AI tools throughout the requirements process, including Otter.ai for interview capture, Dovetail for synthesis, IBM DOORS Next and Requiment for specification management, and GitHub Copilot for downstream implementation traceability.

SE 341 — Agentic Software Engineering (New Course, In Development)

A new NCAAA-compliant undergraduate course specification, structured around the software development life cycle, the foundations of AI agents, and executing a fully agentic SDLC.

Research & Supervision

Research centers on software architecture and quality, the economics and risk of software delivery, and how generative AI is reshaping the discipline — connected throughout by a commitment to humanized computing.

Software Architecture Green & Sustainable Computing AI-Assisted Software Engineering Risk & Cost Estimation Requirements Engineering

Working Papers

The Carbon Clause — proposes carbon reduction as a formal, first-class non-functional (quality) requirement in software engineering, alongside dependability, portability, reliability, and throughput. In preparation, targeting ICSE-level scientific merit.

Regulating the Revolution — examines GenAI's phase-wise impact across the SDLC and junior-developer absorption economics, and proposes a precedent-grounded regulatory framework (drawing on the EU AI Act Art. 14 and SAG-AFTRA 2023), including an auditable Human Contribution Index built from git telemetry and prompt tracing. Under preparation for the ICSE Software Engineering in Society (SEIS) track.

Graduate Supervision

Actively supervising graduate and undergraduate research in the areas above. Prospective students are welcome to reach out at b.shahzad@upm.edu.sa.
